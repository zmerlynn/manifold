# ACT3 Lane C (literature): topologically-robust self-intersection resolution with approximate arithmetic

Deliverable for the Regularize3D campaign. Question: is there a proven "third act" for
Smith-Dodgson-style (approximate arithmetic, guaranteed-manifold output, eps-bounded
geometry) SELF-overlap resolution -- valid oriented self-overlapping soup -> boundary of
{w_S >= 1} -- to replace our one-exact-predicate + SoS regularizer?

Method: web-grounded survey, every claim carries a URL. Paywalled / not-directly-read
items are marked [PAYWALL] or [UNVERIFIED-PROOF-TEXT]. Dates as reported by sources.

Bottom line up front is in the VERDICT section at the end.

================================================================================
1. SMITH + DODGSON (the "never-exact, topology-guaranteed" school our booleans follow)
================================================================================

THESIS (confirmed bibliographic details):
  Julian M. Smith, "Towards robust inexact geometric computation", University of
  Cambridge Computer Laboratory Technical Report UCAM-CL-TR-766, December 2009. Based on
  a PhD dissertation submitted July 2009, St. Edmund's College, Cambridge (advisor Neil
  A. Dodgson).
  - Metadata verified: https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/techreports/UCAM-CL-TR-766.html
  - PDF: https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/techreports/UCAM-CL-TR-766.pdf  (fetched, renders as
    binary via the fetch tool; metadata/abstract confirmed from the .html abstract page)
  Scope per the abstract page: TWO algorithms -- (1) Boolean operations on polygonal/
  polyhedral shapes in BOTH 2D and 3D that are "topologically robust, meaning that they
  are guaranteed to generate a topologically valid result from a topologically valid
  input, irrespective of numerical errors"; (2) a 2D shape-simplification algorithm built
  on a Bentley-Ottmann sweep. This matches our stack: boolean2/CrossSection follows the
  2D sweep (thesis ch. 6-8), boolean3 follows the 3D Boolean.

PAPER:
  J.M. Smith, N.A. Dodgson, "A topologically robust algorithm for Boolean operations on
  polyhedral shapes using approximate arithmetic", Computer-Aided Design 39(2), Feb 2007,
  pp. 149-163.
  - https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0010448506002090  [PAYWALL]

WHAT IS PROVEN (the closure/consistency guarantee):
  The output boundary representation is guaranteed to have VALID CONNECTIVITY (a valid
  2-manifold topology) whenever the two input boundary reps have valid connectivity,
  regardless of the arithmetic used or the magnitude of numerical error. The mechanism:
  the algorithm is built from a small set of INTERRELATED operations whose mutual
  consistency (each decision is forced to agree with the others) prevents the
  topology-breaking contradictions that inconsistent float signs would otherwise cause.
  Geometric artifacts (slivers, zero-length edges, zero-area facets, tiny gaps) are
  tolerated and smoothed in post-processing; only the COMBINATORICS are protected.
  - Abstract-level confirmation via search of the CAD 2007 record and the TR-766 abstract
    page (above). The full proof text is [PAYWALL] / [UNVERIFIED-PROOF-TEXT]; I could not
    read the theorem statements directly.

DOES ANY PROOF STEP RELY ON TWO-OPERAND (bipartite) STRUCTURE?  [UNVERIFIED-PROOF-TEXT --
  reasoned from the formulation, not read from the proof]
  The algorithm classifies each face of shape A against the OTHER solid B (in / out / on-
  boundary) and vice versa; the "interrelated operations" whose consistency is the whole
  proof are precisely these cross-classifications of A-vs-B and B-vs-A. The published
  guarantee is stated for a Boolean of TWO operands, and manifold's boolean3 is likewise
  two-operand. So the consistency argument is expressed over a bipartite A/B partition.
  For SELF-overlap there is no "other solid": the classification target is the integer
  winding number w_S of a single soup and the extraction is boundary-of-{w_S >= 1}. I
  found NO published statement that the Smith consistency proof carries over to single-
  mesh self-union. Treat "the third act is a direct corollary of Smith" as UNPROVEN.

ERRATA / KNOWN FAILURE CASES since 2007:
  No formal erratum found. Follow-on papers characterize the school's KNOWN limitation:
  it guarantees topology but NOT geometric fidelity, so it can output degenerate or self-
  intersecting-in-embedding facets that are combinatorially valid. Later "exact" papers
  (Zhou 2016; Cherchi 2020) motivate themselves partly against this fidelity gap.
  - Cherchi et al. 2020 (below) frames float methods as trading geometric guarantees for
    speed; exact methods as the response.

2. SELF-INTERSECTION / ARRANGEMENT LITERATURE (mostly the "exact-when-needed" school)
================================================================================

Zhou, Grinspun, Zorin, Jacobson, "Mesh Arrangements for Solid Geometry", ACM TOG 35(4),
SIGGRAPH 2016.
  - PDF: http://www.cs.columbia.edu/cg/mesh-arrangements/mesh-arrangements-for-solid-geometry-siggraph-2016-compressed-zhou-et-al.pdf
  - https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/2897824.2925901
  WHY EXACT: the recipe "makes no general position assumptions and does not resort to
  numerical perturbation"; it represents objects in EXACT arithmetic precisely because
  near-degeneracies (coplanar, near-coincident, near-collinear) make float sign decisions
  mutually inconsistent, which is the same realizability problem we measured. It is
  VARIADIC (n-ary), so it already covers "single self-overlapping soup" as the n=1 self-
  arrangement case, and it does interior classification by winding number after building
  an exact arrangement -- i.e. it is the exact-arithmetic version of exactly our target,
  minus the eps-bounded-float-output requirement. Validated on 10k Thingi meshes.

Guo, Fu, "Exact and Efficient Intersection Resolution for Mesh Arrangements", ACM TOG
43(6), Nov 2024.
  - Project: https://mangoleaves.github.io/projects/mesh-arrangements/
  - https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3687925
  Introduces INDIRECT OFFSET PREDICATES to represent intersection points; exact-when-
  needed (filtered, exact only on filter failure); ~1 order of magnitude faster than prior
  state of the art. Same school as Cherchi (below), refined.

Cherchi, Livesu, Scateni, Attene, "Fast and Robust Mesh Arrangements using Floating-point
Arithmetic", ACM TOG 39(6), SIGGRAPH Asia 2020.
  - PDF: https://www.gianmarcocherchi.com/pdf/mesh_arrangement.pdf
  - Code: https://github.com/gcherchi/FastAndRobustMeshArrangements
  Turns ANY set of triangles into a well-formed simplicial complex. Uses Attene's INDIRECT
  PREDICATES: float-expansion, exact-WHEN-NEEDED (exploits FP hardware, falls to exact
  only when the filter is inconclusive). "Robust and guaranteed correct, while virtually
  as fast as non-robust FP implementations." This is the closest published relative of OUR
  school (filter-first, exact fallback) -- NOT the Smith never-exact school.

Cherchi, Pellacini, Attene, Livesu, "Interactive and Robust Mesh Booleans", ACM TOG
41(6), SIGGRAPH Asia 2022.
  - arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.14151
  - Code: https://github.com/gcherchi/InteractiveAndRobustMeshBooleans
  First Boolean pipeline with robustness guarantees at interactive rates (200K tris), same
  indirect-predicate (exact-when-needed) engine. Two-operand/CSG oriented.

Attene, "A lightweight approach to repairing digitized polygon meshes" (MeshFix), The
Visual Computer 2010.
  - PDF: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/220068277_A_lightweight_approach_to_repairing_digitized_polygon_meshes
  - Code: https://github.com/MarcoAttene/MeshFix-V2.1
  The REPAIR school. Removes self-intersections, holes, non-manifold/degenerate elements,
  outputs a single watertight manifold, modifying only LOCALLY near defects. GUARANTEE: a
  manifold watertight output, but it is HEURISTIC -- it does not preserve the input's
  intended solid (it can delete/patch geometry), and gives no eps bound on how far the
  result is from the true boundary-of-{w>=1}. Not a faithful regularizer.

Campen, Kobbelt, "Exact and Robust (Self-)Intersections for Polygonal Meshes",
Eurographics 2010 (plane-based / BSP localization).
  - Referenced widely; EXACT with plane-based (Nef-style) representation + BSP + a
    localization/octree scheme. Preconditions reported for the classic PGBT variant:
    inputs closed, manifold, correctly oriented, non-degenerate, and actually intersecting
    (neither disjoint nor nested) -- i.e. two-operand oriented.
  - https://www.graphics.rwth-aachen.de/media/papers/339/ember_exact_mesh_booleans_via_efficient_and_robust_local_arrangements.pdf
    (EMBER, Trettner/Kobbelt, TOG 2022 -- exact mesh Booleans via efficient/robust LOCAL
    arrangements, octree-embedded BSP; https://dl.acm.org/doi/abs/10.1145/3528223.3530181)

CGAL 3D Nef polyhedra (Selective Nef Complexes): Granados, Hachenberger, Hert, Kettner,
Mehlhorn, Seel, ESA 2003; Hachenberger PhD 2006.
  - https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-540-39658-1_59
  - Thesis PDF: https://publikationen.sulb.uni-saarland.de/bitstream/20.500.11880/25961/1/Dissertation_1778_Hach_Pete_2006.pdf
  Fully general, EXACT, handles all degeneracies incl. non-manifold / mixed-dimensional /
  open-closed. The "gold standard for correctness"; reported SLOW and very memory-heavy.
  Exact school, not float-topology-guaranteed.

WINDING-NUMBER INTERIOR CLASSIFICATION (the classifier, orthogonal to the arrangement):
  Jacobson, Kavan, Sorkine-Hornung, "Robust Inside-Outside Segmentation using Generalized
  Winding Numbers", ACM TOG 32(4), SIGGRAPH 2013.
  - PDF: https://igl.ethz.ch/projects/winding-number/robust-inside-outside-segmentation-using-generalized-winding-numbers-siggraph-2013-jacobson-et-al.pdf
  Barill, Dickson, Schmidt, Levin, Jacobson, "Fast Winding Numbers for Soups and Clouds",
  ACM TOG 37(4), SIGGRAPH 2018 (4-way BVH, ~1000x speedup).
  - PDF: https://www.dgp.toronto.edu/projects/fast-winding-numbers/fast-winding-numbers-for-soups-and-clouds-siggraph-2018-compressed-barill-et-al.pdf
  WHAT THEY CLAIM re robustness at near-degeneracy: the generalized winding number is a
  HARMONIC field that "smoothly degrades" through self-intersections/holes/non-manifold
  input; it stays meaningful "until the mesh breaks consistent orientation assumptions."
  KEY CAVEAT for us: this is robustness of a CONTINUOUS SCALAR CLASSIFIER, not a guarantee
  of a MANIFOLD extracted output. It answers "how inside is this point" continuously; it
  does NOT by itself produce a topologically-valid boundary mesh, and near a coincident
  face the field is ambiguous (half-integer). So winding numbers give us the correct
  classification target (w_S) but no manifold-extraction guarantee -- they are the
  semantics, not the third act.
  (Recent follow-ups: Barill/Martens 2025 "One-Shot" and 2026 "Antipodal Method" push
  accuracy/speed, same classifier semantics; not robustness-of-topology claims.)

SELF-OVERLAP-SPECIFIC (closest to our exact problem statement):
  Gagniere et al., "A Robust Grid-Based Meshing Algorithm for Embedding Self-Intersecting
  Surfaces", CGF 2024. https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.06256
  Resolves self-intersection/self-OVERLAP by keeping MULTIPLE COPIES of a grid cell in
  overlapped regions, deliberately MINIMIZING exact/adaptive arithmetic. It embeds (for
  simulation), it does not extract boundary-of-{w>=1}; different deliverable, but it is
  direct evidence that self-overlap is being attacked with "mostly float + tiny exact."

3. SNAP ROUNDING (output-perturbation; the only school that natively targets FLOAT output)
================================================================================

2D theory (solid, manifold-preserving):
  Hobby (1999) and Guibas-Marimont snap rounding round an arrangement's vertices to a grid
  while keeping combinatorial structure; iterated snap rounding (Halperin-Packer) bounds
  vertex-edge distance. Overview: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snap_rounding
  2D snap rounding is well understood and can preserve a valid planar subdivision.

3D theory (the known blowup / difficulty result -- load-bearing):
  Fortune gave the first 3D snap rounding but with input requirements that make it
  impractical. Devillers, Lazard, Lieutier, "3D Snap Rounding", SoCG 2018.
  - https://inria.hal.science/hal-01698928/document
  It is reported as "the first and only working [3D] algorithm with guarantees", but with
  impractically high polynomial complexity (reported ~O(n^19)); and a 3D subdivision of
  complexity n can blow up to complexity O(n^4) under rounding. So a provable, general,
  manifold-preserving 3D snap-rounding arrangement EXISTS on paper but is not usable.
  - complexity figures per the search summary of the Devillers/Valque line
    [UNVERIFIED exact exponent from primary PDF; directionally: "impractically high"].

Practical 3D snap for meshes (the deployable near-relative of the third act):
  Valque, Lazard, "Resolving self-intersections in 3D meshes while preserving floating-
  point coordinates", Computer Graphics Forum 44(5), 2025 (e70197). Also Valque PhD thesis
  (Univ. Lorraine, 2024) and Inria HAL preprint.
  - CGF: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/cgf.70197  [PAYWALL]
  - HAL preprint: https://inria.hal.science/hal-04907149  (bot-blocked on fetch)
  - Thesis: https://docnum.univ-lorraine.fr/public/DDOC_T_2024_0337_VALQUE.pdf
  - HAL v1: https://hal.science/hal-05242294v1
  Shipped as CGAL's autorefine + snap-rounding feature (verified fetch):
  - https://www.cgal.org/2025/06/13/autorefine-and-snap/
  METHOD: (1) autorefine_triangle_soup() resolves ALL self-intersections of a SINGLE soup
  EXACTLY (splits triangles so nothing intersects except along shared edge/vertex); (2)
  snap-round vertices to an integer grid, drop degeneracies, RE-autorefine, ITERATE until
  intersection-free. GUARANTEE DELIVERED: intersection-free triangle soup with double-
  precision float coordinates; resolved ALL 9,997 Thingi10K self-intersecting models (most
  in one iteration; naive rounding alone left 572 unresolved).
  STATED NON-GUARANTEES (from the CGAL page): NO theoretical termination proof; degenerate
  faces may be created during rounding; input may be non-manifold and OUTPUT MANIFOLDNESS
  IS NOT GUARANTEED; no per-vertex displacement bound is claimed (grid resolution is the
  only lever).
  CLASSIFICATION: this is EXACT-WHEN-NEEDED (exact autorefine) + OUTPUT SNAP ROUNDING. It
  is the nearest DEPLOYABLE thing to a self-overlap third act -- single-soup in, float
  intersection-free out -- but it RESOLVES intersections, it does not REGULARIZE to
  boundary-of-{w>=1}, and it does not promise a manifold. It is not Smith's never-exact
  school; it uses exact arithmetic internally.

4. SYMBOLIC PERTURBATION vs CONTROLLED PERTURBATION (the realizability question)
================================================================================

SoS / symbolic perturbation:
  Edelsbrunner, Mucke, "Simulation of Simplicity", ACM TOG 9(1) 1990.
  - https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9410209
  - https://www.geom.uiuc.edu/~mucke/GeomDir/sos90.html
  SoS adds symbolic infinitesimals so no predicate ever returns exactly zero, giving a
  consistent tie-break for EXACTLY-degenerate configs. This is what our current regularizer
  uses (one exact predicate + SoS).
  THE LIMITATION FOR NEAR-DEGENERATE (not exactly degenerate) INPUT: SoS only rescues the
  case where the predicate's TRUE value is exactly 0 and you want a consistent nonzero
  tie-break. When the input is float and the TRUE geometry is merely uncertain (predicate
  value within the error bound of 0 but sign genuinely unknown), SoS does nothing to make
  the chosen signs JOINTLY REALIZABLE -- exactly the realizability gap the campaign
  measured. Formalization of that gap:

  Salesin, Stolfi, Guibas, "Epsilon Geometry: building robust algorithms from imprecise
  computations", SoCG 1989. https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/73833.73857
  This is the canonical formalization of "a set of predicate signs can be combinatorially
  derivable yet NOT REALIZABLE by any nearby geometry". Their fix is to test consistency of
  each sweep step and MODIFY THE INPUT so the output is realizable by curves close to the
  input -- i.e. epsilon-geometry already points at input perturbation as the resolution of
  the realizability gap. Directly names our measured phenomenon.
  (Yap's EGC / "exact geometric computation" line is the opposite pole: make predicates
  exact so realizability is never violated. Robustness surveys: Yap, Mehlhorn-Yap.)

CONTROLLED PERTURBATION (the "perturb INPUT until all plain-float predicates are certain"
school -- the candidate third act by construction):
  Halperin, Leiserowitz, "Controlled perturbation for arrangements of circles", SoCG 2003.
  http://www.math.tau.ac.il/~danha/publications.html
  Raab, "Controlled perturbation for arrangements of polyhedral surfaces with application
  to swept volumes", SoCG 1999 (the 3D polyhedral-surface case). Same list.
  Funke, Klein, Mehlhorn, Schmitt, "Controlled perturbation for Delaunay triangulations",
  SODA 2005. http://graphics.stanford.edu/~sfunke/Papers/SODA05/CP-paper.pdf
  Mehlhorn, Osbild, Sagraloff, "A General Approach to the Analysis of Controlled
  Perturbation Algorithms", CGTA 2011.
  https://people.mpi-inf.mpg.de/~mehlhorn/ftp/ControlledPerturbationGeneralApproach.pdf
  IDEA: replace input x by a random x' in a delta-neighborhood, then run the PLAIN-FLOAT
  idealized algorithm on x'. Choose delta and float precision L so that, with constant
  probability, EVERY predicate that arises is evaluated FAR from its zero set (each guarded
  predicate is certified by plain floats). The theory gives a relation between delta
  (perturbation magnitude), L (precision), and a "resolution bound" that depends on the
  predicate degree and how crowded/near-degenerate the instance is. It provably converts an
  idealized algorithm into a plain-float one -- and Raab's variant already covers
  arrangements of POLYHEDRAL SURFACES.

5. CLASSIFICATION OF EVERY APPROACH ON THE OWNER'S AXIS
================================================================================

  exact-when-needed (OUR school: filter with floats, exact only on filter failure):
    - manifold RemoveOverlaps3D current (one exact predicate + SoS)   <-- us
    - Zhou et al. 2016 (rational)          [exact always, no perturbation; strictest]
    - Cherchi et al. 2020 / 2022, Guo-Fu 2024 (Attene indirect predicates)  <-- nearest kin
    - Campen-Kobbelt 2010, EMBER 2022, CGAL Nef (exact plane-based / BSP)
    - CGAL autorefine (exact autorefine) + Valque-Lazard snap  <-- exact core + snap output

  never-exact, topology-guaranteed (SMITH school: plain floats forever, consistency proof):
    - Smith 2007 / thesis 2009; manifold boolean2 + boolean3   <-- the model the owner wants
    (No member of this school is published for SINGLE-MESH self-union.)

  input-perturbation (CONTROLLED PERTURBATION school: move input into general position):
    - Halperin-Leiserowitz 2003 (circles), Raab 1999 (polyhedral surfaces),
      Funke-Mehlhorn 2005 (Delaunay), Mehlhorn-Osbild-Sagraloff 2011 (general analysis)
    - epsilon-geometry (Salesin-Stolfi-Guibas 1989) is the proto/realizability formalization

  repair / heuristic (no faithful guarantee):
    - Attene MeshFix 2010; generalized-winding classifiers (Jacobson 2013, Barill 2018)
      are the classifier semantics, not an extraction guarantee.

VERDICT
================================================================================

(a) DOES A PROVEN THIRD ACT EXIST?  NO.
  I found no paper that PROVES a Smith-style guarantee -- guaranteed-manifold output from
  approximate arithmetic with eps-bounded geometry -- for SINGLE-MESH self-overlap
  regularization (boundary-of-{w_S >= 1}). Smith's proofs are stated and shipped as
  TWO-operand Booleans; the consistency argument is expressed over an A-vs-B partition and
  nobody has published its extension to self-union (marked [UNVERIFIED-PROOF-TEXT], CAD
  2007 paywalled). The exact schools (Zhou 2016, Cherchi/Guo-Fu, CGAL) DO solve single-
  soup arrangement + winding classification with GUARANTEED topology, but via exact
  arithmetic, not never-exact floats. The one thing that ships "single soup in, float
  intersection-free out" (Valque-Lazard / CGAL autorefine+snap, 2025) explicitly does NOT
  guarantee manifoldness and has no termination proof. So the exact triple (never-exact OR
  eps-float, guaranteed manifold, faithful {w>=1}) is not jointly proven anywhere.

(b) CLOSEST SCHOOL + SMALLEST GAP.
  Two honest answers depending on which requirement is hard.
  - If "never-exact floats" is the hard requirement: the SMITH school is closest, and the
    smallest gap is a MATH gap -- re-derive the interrelated-operations consistency proof
    with the classification target changed from binary in/out-of-the-other-solid to
    integer winding-parity of one soup. This is research-grade, not a corollary.
  - If "float output + guaranteed manifold" is the hard requirement and "never-exact" is
    negotiable: the EXACT-WHEN-NEEDED / snap family is closest and is nearly deployable --
    CGAL autorefine + Valque-Lazard snap already gives single-soup float intersection-free
    output; the remaining gap is (i) add winding classification to extract {w>=1}, and
    (ii) upgrade "intersection-free" to "manifold" (a small closing/orientation pass), both
    on an exact core rather than plain floats. This is the lower-risk path.

(c) CONTROLLED PERTURBATION FOR OUR PIPELINE.
  It IS the realizability fix by construction -- the perturbed input is a nearby REALIZABLE
  instance where all plain-float predicates are certified, which is exactly the sign-
  assignment-not-jointly-satisfiable failure we measured (epsilon-geometry names it). And
  Raab already handles arrangements of polyhedral surfaces, so self-overlap is in scope in
  principle (perturb the single soup to general position, run idealized winding extraction
  on floats). BUT four concrete frictions against our contract:
   1. MAGNITUDE vs OUR EPS: the perturbation delta is governed by a resolution/precision
      bound (delta-vs-L relation) tied to predicate degree and how CROWDED the instance is.
      Our corpus is built on EXACT coincidences (welds, shared faces) -- the worst case for
      crowding -- so the delta needed to certify every predicate is a worst-case/
      probabilistic bound, NOT a per-vertex eps we control; it can exceed a tight eps
      contract precisely where we have the most coincident geometry.
   2. IT PERTURBS AWAY THE SIGNAL: for a self-overlap regularizer the coincident/overlapping
      faces ARE the signal that w_S counts. Controlled perturbation separates them into
      general position; the extracted boundary is only eps-close and the exact coincidence
      structure our machinery treats as meaningful is destroyed.
   3. RANDOMIZED / LAS VEGAS: it re-perturbs and retries on guard failure. That conflicts
      with our DETERMINISTIC once-only provenance -- retries change coordinates globally and
      would need to be re-threaded through provenance each attempt.
   4. NO MANIFOLD GUARANTEE OF ITS OWN: CP only guarantees the float run behaves AS IF in
      general position; output manifoldness is inherited from the idealized algorithm, so it
      does not by itself supply the guarantee the owner wants -- it removes degeneracy, it
      does not certify topology.
  NET: controlled perturbation is the right diagnosis of the realizability gap and is
  applicable to self-overlap in principle, but it composes poorly with our eps contract,
  our coincidence-as-signal semantics, and our deterministic once-only provenance. It is
  better read as EVIDENCE for the exact-when-needed / snap path (b) than as a drop-in third
  act.
