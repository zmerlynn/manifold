## THE CENTRAL EMPIRICAL QUESTION

The cap-snap works because near-coplanar caps sit within eps of a common plane over
their whole footprint (pairGap < eps), so the global-planarity guard admits them. The
r4/r5 measurement says the wall clusters' triple points spread 1e-4 down to ~2*eps.
If the near-parallel WALL pair diverges by ~1e-4 (>> eps ~ 3.5e-10) across the strip,
then snapping them to a common carrier moves vertices by ~1e-4 and the eps guard MUST
fail closed (or, forced past it, breaks the epsilon-valid contract + resolving
carriers). So the viability of ROUTE-B hinges on ONE measurement: for the wall pairs
that generate the sub-eps thin cells, what per-vertex displacement makes them exactly
coincident, and is it <= eps? Measure before building.

there is no common carrier within eps to snap onto.
