## THE CENTRAL EMPIRICAL QUESTION

The cap-snap works because near-coplanar caps sit within eps of a common plane over
their whole footprint (pairGap < eps), so the global-planarity guard admits them. The
r4/r5 measurement says the wall clusters' triple points spread 1e-4 down to ~2*eps.
If the near-parallel WALL pair diverges by ~1e-4 (>> eps ~ 3.5e-10) across the strip,
then snapping them to a common carrier moves vertices by ~1e-4 and the eps guard MUST
fail closed (or, forced past it, breaks the epsilon-valid contract + resolving
carriers). So the viability of ROUTE-B hinges on ONE measurement: for the wall pairs
that generate the sub-eps thin cells, what per-vertex displacement makes them exactly
coincident, and is it <= eps? Measure before building.

CONCLUSION (the central question, answered): the near-parallel walls that generate
the sub-eps thin cells diverge by 29000x to 64,000,000x eps. The ONLY input-side
operation that removes a thin cell is making the two generating walls COINCIDENT
(exactly parallel is not enough - two distinct parallel planes still cut two
distinct near-coincident lines on the near-tangent face, so the cell persists). That
coincidence snap moves geometry by 4 to 7 ORDERS OF MAGNITUDE beyond the eps guard.
No within-eps input-side plane/line snap exists. ROUTE-B's premise ("parallel-and-
coincident along a strip, snap within eps like the caps") does NOT hold here: the
walls are near-parallel but their COINCIDENCE gap is macroscopic (1e-5..2e-2), NOT
sub-eps. The near-coincident SEAM LINES on face f arise from f being near-TANGENT to
both walls (ill-conditioned crossing), not from the walls being near-coincident - so
there is no common carrier within eps to snap onto.
